# North Star: Decision Quality

Every company that builds tools for investors eventually has to answer one question honestly: what are you actually optimizing for? Most never answer it out loud, because the honest answer is embarrassing — trades executed, screens viewed, hours in app, subscriptions renewed. Aegis answers it here, in writing, so that every future argument about features, metrics, and roadmaps can be settled by pointing at this page.

**Our north star is decision quality.** Not returns. Not engagement. Not intelligence for its own sake. The single question against which every feature, every model, every pixel is judged: *did this make the user's capital allocation decisions measurably better?*

## Why decision quality, not outcomes

Investing is a domain where outcomes lie. A reckless decision can pay off spectacularly; a rigorous one can lose money for years before being vindicated — or never be vindicated at all. If we optimized for outcomes, we would be optimizing for luck, and we would teach our users to worship luck too. That is what most of the industry already does, and it is why most individual investors churn through strategies, chase performance, and quietly underperform the indexes they could have bought for free.

Adaptive Quality Investing rests on a harder, truer claim: over long horizons, the quality of your decision *process* is the only thing you control, and the only thing that compounds reliably. Price prediction is a commodity fantasy. Process quality is a craft. Aegis exists to industrialize that craft for the individual.

## What does not count as success

We name these explicitly because each one is a gravitational field that has captured better-funded companies than us.

**Short-term outperformance does not count.** If a cohort of Aegis users beats the market for two quarters, that is noise. We will not market it, we will not build features to chase it, and we will not let it distort the roadmap. A user who beats the market by abandoning their thesis discipline has been failed by us, whatever their brokerage statement says.

**Engagement does not count.** Time-in-app is, past a threshold, a *negative* signal for a long-term investor. The user who logs in daily to stare at prices is being harmed. We will never ship a feature whose primary effect is to increase session frequency. Notifications exist to surface thesis-relevant evidence, not to manufacture urgency. If Aegis is working, a user might open it a few times a month — and every one of those sessions changes what they know or what they do.

**Feature count does not count.** Screeners have ten thousand filters and produce no wisdom. Depth of features is a cost we pay only when it purchases decision quality. Every feature proposal must answer: which decision does this improve, and how would we know?

## What does count

**Consistency of process.** A quality decision follows a knowable path: thesis stated, evidence gathered, risks named, catalysts identified, decision recorded with reasoning intact. Success is the fraction of a user's capital deployed through that path rather than around it. When a user who used to buy on a headline now cannot imagine buying without a written case, we have succeeded.

**Explainability of every decision.** At any moment, for any holding, the user should be able to answer: why do I own this, what would change my mind, and what has changed since I decided? If Aegis cannot reconstruct the reasoning behind a decision — including our own system's contributions to it — that is a defect of the same severity as data loss. An unexplainable decision is not a decision; it is a bet wearing a decision's clothes.

**Evidence quality.** Decisions are only as good as what they rest on. We measure whether the evidence attached to a thesis is primary rather than derivative, dated and sourced rather than vibes, disconfirming as well as confirming. A thesis file that contains only bullish evidence is an alarm, not an asset, and the product should treat it as one.

**Learning over time.** The individual investor's greatest structural disadvantage is not information — it is the absence of a feedback loop. Institutions run post-mortems; individuals repeat mistakes for decades because nothing forces them to notice. Aegis closes the loop: every decision becomes a learning event, every outcome is reconciled against the reasoning that produced it, and the user's behavior profile makes their own patterns — good and bad — visible to them. Success is a user whose tenth investment case is visibly, structurally better than their first, and who can show you exactly why.

## Holding the line

This north star will be attacked, mostly by ourselves. There will be a quarter when engagement metrics would flatter a fundraise. There will be a bull market when users demand price targets and a competitor supplies them. There will be a plausible-sounding argument that "we can measure decision quality *through* engagement." Each of these is the same temptation in different clothes: substituting what is easy to measure for what is true.

The discipline we ask of our users — hold the thesis, weigh the evidence, ignore the noise — is the discipline we demand of ourselves. Decision quality is the metric. Everything else is instrumentation.
