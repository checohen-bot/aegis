# The Customer: The Serious Individual Investor

Aegis is built for one customer, described here in enough depth that every product decision can be tested against them. When we say "the user," this is who we mean — and everyone we do not mean is listed too, because a customer definition that excludes no one defines no one.

## Who they are

Our customer is an individual making real capital allocation decisions with their own money, on a horizon measured in years. They might be an engineer with equity compensation to redeploy, a physician fifteen years from retirement, a small-business owner with proceeds to invest, a diligent professional who reads annual reports on weekends. Demographics vary; disposition does not. The defining traits:

**They think in ownership, not in trades.** They want to own good businesses at sensible prices and hold them while the thesis remains true. They know what a 10-K is even if they don't read every one. They have heard of Buffett's punch card and felt the idea land.

**The money is theirs, and it means something.** This is retirement, children's education, hard-won savings. Losses are not a drawdown statistic; they are years of work. This makes our customer appropriately serious — and appropriately skeptical of anyone selling certainty.

**They are self-directed by conviction, not by default.** They could hand the money to an advisor or index it entirely — and often index part of it. The portion they run themselves, they run because they believe thoughtful ownership of businesses they understand is both rational and worth doing. They want to get *better* at it, not have it done for them.

**They are honest enough to know their own gaps.** They have chased at least one stock, held at least one loser too long out of loyalty, and sold at least one winner too early out of fear. Unlike the tourist, they noticed.

## Who they are not

Not institutions — those have Bloomberg, analysts, and committees. Not day traders or momentum chasers — Aegis is actively hostile terrain for them, by design. Not passive-only investors with nothing self-directed — we admire them, but they need nothing we make. Not gamblers seeking action — every design choice we make will bore them, deliberately.

## What they lack

Strip away the noise and our customer lacks four structural things:

1. **A process container.** Their reasoning lives in their head, scattered notes, maybe a spreadsheet. Nothing enforces the discipline they intellectually endorse: state the thesis, gather evidence, name the risks, record the decision.
2. **Institutional memory.** They cannot answer "what did I believe when I bought this, and what has changed?" for their own portfolio. Every market panic is fought from scratch, against their own amnesia.
3. **An evidence engine.** Relevant information exists — filings, transcripts, disclosures — but arrives as an undifferentiated flood. No one connects a specific development to *their specific thesis* and says: this matters to you, and here is why.
4. **A feedback loop.** No mechanism turns outcomes into learning. Mistakes repeat for decades because nothing forces reconciliation between what they believed and what happened.

## Why existing tools fail them

**Screeners and data terminals** hand them a firehose and call it empowerment. They answer "what are the numbers?" — never "what should I believe, and on what grounds?" All library, no librarian; the synthesis burden stays entirely on the user, which is precisely the burden they needed lifted.

**Robo-advisors** solve a different problem for a different person. They allocate; they do not deepen judgment. For our customer, a robo-advisor is capitulation — "stop thinking, we'll handle it" — when what they want is to think *better*.

**Trading apps** are engineered against this customer's interests. Their revenue scales with activity, so everything — confetti, streaks, push notifications, price-first design — manufactures the impulsivity our customer is trying to outgrow. Using a trading app to practice long-term investing is doing surgery in a casino.

**Chatbots and AI assistants** produce fluent, confident, sourceless answers with no memory of the user's theses, no accountability for past claims, and no structure connecting one answer to the next. Our customer needs reasoning that persists, connects, and can be audited — not eloquence on demand.

Each category fails the same way: none of them holds the user's *reasoning* as a first-class object. They hold data, or orders, or allocations, or conversations. Nobody holds the thinking.

## What being served well looks like

Served well, our customer experiences something closer to institutional practice than retail software. Every holding has a living investment case they can reread. New evidence finds *them*, pre-connected to what they own and believe, with sources attached. When markets panic, they reopen their own reasoning instead of refreshing prices — and act from the thesis, whichever direction it points. When they decide, the decision is recorded with its full context, and months later the outcome is reconciled against it. Over years, they watch their own judgment visibly improve, with the evidence of that improvement in front of them.

And crucially: they check in a few times a month, not ten times a day. Aegis measures its success by the quality of their decisions and the calm of their conviction — never by their attention. That inversion — serving the investor instead of consuming them — is the entire relationship, and it is one no incumbent can offer without unmaking itself.
