# Personas

Aegis serves individual investors who take their own capital seriously and want to allocate it better. It does not serve traders, speculators, or investors seeking signals to follow. The personas below are the concrete people V1 is built for. Each is defined by their goals, their frustration with the tools they use today, and what Aegis specifically changes for them.

These are design instruments, not marketing segments. When a decision is contested, we ask what it does for these three people.

## Persona 1 — The Disciplined Accumulator

**Who:** Maya, 41, a professional in a demanding non-finance career. She has invested consistently for fifteen years and holds twelve to eighteen positions in an IBKR account. She reads company filings and earnings transcripts when she can. She thinks in years and is genuinely unbothered by quarterly swings.

**Goals:** Maya wants to be sure her convictions are still valid. She bought each position for a reason, but some of those reasons are years old and live only in her memory or a scattered note. She wants to know, per Holding, whether the original thesis still holds — and to be told when something material actually changes, without having to monitor everything herself.

**Frustration with existing tools:** Her brokerage shows her prices and P&L, which she finds nearly useless — they tell her what happened, never whether her reasoning is intact. Financial media manufactures constant urgency about things that do not matter to a decade-long holder. Screeners and analyst-rating aggregators give her verdicts with no visible reasoning she can weigh. Nothing she uses remembers why she bought.

**What Aegis does for her:** Aegis attaches an explicit Investment Thesis to each Holding and tracks Evidence and Observations against it continuously. It stays silent through noise and speaks only when an Observation genuinely bears on one of her theses, framed as confirm-or-challenge rather than buy-or-sell. When she acts, the Decision and its reasoning are recorded, so next year's Maya inherits this year's Maya's thinking. For her, Aegis is the memory and vigilance her discipline already deserves.

## Persona 2 — The First-Process Investor

**Who:** Daniel, 29, three years into investing with real intent after a period of following tips and reacting to headlines. He holds six positions, some inherited from that earlier phase without a clear rationale. He is intelligent, motivated, and aware that he lacks a method.

**Goals:** Daniel wants to build a real process and stop making decisions he cannot defend to himself. He wants to learn what a sound investment case actually consists of, and to see his own patterns — including his mistakes — clearly enough to correct them. He is looking for structure, not a shortcut.

**Frustration with existing tools:** Everything aimed at him is either gamified — treating investing as a game with streaks and confetti that reward activity over judgment — or it is professional-grade terminals that assume expertise he does not yet have. The first set trains bad habits; the second is unusable. Nothing teaches him how to think; they only make it easier to transact.

**What Aegis does for him:** Aegis gives Daniel scaffolding for judgment. When he forms a Thesis, the workspace guides him toward the components of a real Investment Case — what has to be true, what would disprove it, what Evidence supports it — turning a vague hunch into an explicit, inspectable structure. Because every Decision records its reasoning, his early choices become Learning Events, and over time a Behavior Profile shows him honestly where his judgment is strong and where it drifts. Aegis refuses to gamify, so it never rewards him for the activity that would undermine the process he is trying to build. For Daniel, Aegis is the apprenticeship the market never offered.

## Persona 3 — The Consolidating Veteran

**Who:** Priya, 56, two decades of serious self-directed investing across roughly thirty positions. Her research is real but scattered — spreadsheets, a notes app, PDFs, saved articles, and a great deal held only in her head. She has strong judgment and a poor system of record.

**Goals:** Priya wants one authoritative place where her thinking lives, so that her research is retrievable, her decisions are auditable, and nothing depends on remembering where she filed it. She wants to see her full portfolio through the lens of her own theses, and to leave a coherent record — for herself, and for the family who will one day inherit these holdings.

**Frustration with existing tools:** Her knowledge is fragmented across a dozen tools, none of which connect a piece of Evidence to the Decision it informed. When she revisits a position, she cannot reconstruct why she sized it as she did. Her portfolio tools track money; none track reasoning. The intellectual work of decades is scattered and, functionally, unindexed.

**What Aegis does for her:** Aegis consolidates Priya's scattered research into a structured Evidence and Knowledge library, bound to the Companies and Theses it concerns. Her IBKR portfolio syncs, and each Holding becomes a workspace where her accumulated reasoning finally has a home. The Decision log gives her the auditable record she has always wanted, and Knowledge accrued across her portfolio becomes a durable asset rather than a collection of loose files. For Priya, Aegis converts twenty years of dispersed intelligence into one coherent, inheritable system of record.

## What the personas share

All three are long-term public-equity investors who want to make better decisions, not more of them. None want signals to obey; all want reasoning they can own. Aegis is designed for the space these three occupy — and deliberately not for anyone whose goal is speed, frequency, or the outsourcing of judgment.
