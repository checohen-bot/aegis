# Evidence and Observation

Two canonical objects carry the factual load in AQI, and keeping them distinct is essential to the methodology. An **Observation** is a noted change or event about a Company or the world, *not yet evaluated* against a Thesis. **Evidence** is a discrete, sourced fact or data point that *bears on* a specific Thesis or Investment Case. The difference is not the raw fact — it is whether the fact has been evaluated for relevance to a belief. This chapter defines both, the promotion path from one to the other, and the standards that qualify a fact as valid Evidence.

## Why the distinction matters

Most information reaching an investor is undirected: an earnings print, a competitor's product launch, a regulatory filing, a management departure, a macro data release. This is the raw stream of Observations. If everything in the stream were treated as Evidence, the Investment Case would fill with noise, and the investor would confuse *activity* with *analysis*. Conversely, if the stream were ignored, the Thesis would go stale and adaptation would fail.

The Observation-to-Evidence pipeline is the mechanism that keeps AQI both open to new information and disciplined about what it lets influence a belief. Observations are captured cheaply and broadly; Evidence is admitted narrowly and deliberately. This asymmetry is intentional.

## Observations: capture without commitment

An Observation records that *something happened or changed*, attached to a Company (or to the world/sector), timestamped, and sourced, but **not yet interpreted**. Observations have low admission cost by design — the goal is broad, timely capture so that nothing potentially material is lost.

An Observation carries:
- **What changed**: the event or shift, stated factually.
- **Subject**: the Company, sector, or macro context it concerns.
- **Time**: when it occurred and when it was noted.
- **Source**: where it came from.

Critically, an Observation has *no assigned significance*. "Company X's CFO resigned" is an Observation. Whether that matters depends entirely on which Theses reference Company X and what their load-bearing assumptions are. The same Observation may be decisive for one Thesis, irrelevant to another, and confirming to a third. Significance is assigned only at promotion, against a specific Thesis.

Observations may also exist with no live Thesis attached at all — an accumulating record of a Company on a watch list. These feed Knowledge and may later seed the Formation of a new Thesis.

## Promotion: from Observation to Evidence

An Observation becomes Evidence when it is **evaluated against a specific Thesis and found to bear on it.** Promotion is the analytical act at the heart of adaptation. It answers three questions:

1. **Relevance** — Does this Observation bear on a *load-bearing assumption* or an *invalidation condition* of the Thesis? An Observation that touches only incidental positives is noted but not promoted.
2. **Direction** — Does it *confirm* or *disconfirm* the assumption it bears on? Both directions are promoted; disconfirming Evidence is not only admitted but actively sought, because an Investment Case with no disconfirming Evidence is treated as incomplete.
3. **Weight** — How strongly does it bear, given its source quality, recency, and directness? Weight is recorded, not just polarity.

On promotion, the resulting Evidence is attached to the Investment Case, mapped to the specific assumption it addresses, and the Thesis's Quality Framework profile is re-computed. Promotion can trigger a lifecycle transition — most importantly, disconfirming Evidence crossing a materiality threshold routes the Thesis into Challenge or Invalidation.

The promotion decision is itself recorded. An Observation that was *considered and not promoted* is retained with that judgment, so the reasoning trail shows not only what influenced a Thesis but what was consciously set aside — which is itself Evidence of process quality and a hedge against later hindsight distortion.

## What qualifies as valid Evidence

Not every promoted fact is *good* Evidence. Validity is assessed on four standards, which correspond to the Evidence Quality dimension of the Quality Framework:

**Sourcing.** Evidence must be traceable to an identifiable source, and the source's reliability is recorded. A primary source (filing, direct disclosure, first-hand data) outranks secondary reporting, which outranks unattributed commentary. Circular market chatter — "the stock is down so something must be wrong" — is not admissible Evidence; it is at most an Observation about price.

**Recency and validity window.** Evidence has a natural expiry. A supply-constraint data point valid this quarter may be stale next quarter. Evidence is timestamped and, where its relevance decays, given a validity window. Stale Evidence is not silently trusted; it is flagged for refresh, and an Investment Case resting on expired Evidence is downgraded until refreshed.

**Relevance.** Valid Evidence bears on a load-bearing element of the Thesis. Evidence that is true, well-sourced, and current but immaterial to the Thesis's spine is decorative and is not allowed to inflate the Case's apparent strength.

**Independence.** Multiple pieces of Evidence tracing to the same underlying source are not independent confirmation. AQI records the provenance chain so that ten reports of one rumor are not mistaken for ten facts. Genuine confirmation requires independent sourcing.

## Evidence, the Case, and the Thesis

The relationship is layered and precise:
- A **Thesis** is a falsifiable belief with load-bearing assumptions.
- An **Investment Case** is the structured argument for or against that Thesis at a point in time, composed of Evidence mapped to assumptions.
- **Evidence** is the individual sourced fact within the Case.
- **Observations** are the upstream stream from which Evidence is selectively promoted.

Because the Case is explicitly time-stamped ("at a point in time"), AQI maintains a *history* of Cases as Evidence accumulates and expires. This lets the investor — and Aegis — reconstruct not just what the current argument is, but how the argument evolved: which Evidence entered, which expired, which was promoted from Observation and when. That reconstructable evidentiary history is what makes a Thesis's adaptation auditable and what feeds the Learning Loop when the Thesis is finally confirmed or invalidated.
