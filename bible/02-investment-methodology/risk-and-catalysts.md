# Risks and Catalysts

A Thesis is a claim about an uncertain future. Two canonical objects make that uncertainty explicit and trackable. A **Risk** is an identified factor that could invalidate a Thesis or harm a Holding. A **Catalyst** is an anticipated future event that could confirm or invalidate a Thesis. Together they convert a Thesis from a static statement into a *monitored hypothesis*: Risks define what could go wrong, Catalysts define what would resolve the uncertainty, and both are tracked continuously against active Holdings. This chapter defines how they are identified, structured, and used to monitor a Portfolio over time.

## Risks: naming what could break the Thesis

A Risk is identified during Thesis Formation and refined throughout the lifecycle. Its purpose is to make the Thesis's fragility explicit *before* the investor is emotionally committed to the Holding. Every load-bearing assumption implies at least one Risk — the Risk that the assumption is or becomes false.

A well-formed Risk records:
- **The factor**: the specific condition that could harm the Thesis or Holding (e.g., "a well-capitalized entrant compresses pricing," "the key patent is invalidated," "customer concentration converts to revenue loss").
- **The assumption it threatens**: which load-bearing element of the Thesis it would undermine. A Risk not tied to a load-bearing assumption is either mis-specified or points to an assumption the Thesis failed to make explicit.
- **Materialization signals**: the observable states of the world that would indicate the Risk is beginning to occur. These are the specific Observations to watch for.
- **Severity**: whether materialization would *dent*, *narrow*, or *break* the Thesis. A Risk whose materialization meets a pre-committed **invalidation condition** is a break-level Risk and is linked directly to the Thesis's invalidation criteria from the Thesis Lifecycle.

Risks are not one-time inputs. As Observations arrive and are evaluated, materialization signals are matched against the identified Risks. A Risk moves through states — **latent** (identified, not occurring), **materializing** (signals appearing), and **realized** (the factor has occurred) — and each transition can trigger a lifecycle event: a materializing Risk routes the Thesis into Challenge; a realized break-level Risk routes it into Invalidation.

Two categories deserve explicit tracking. **Thesis Risks** threaten the reasoning (the moat erodes, the growth stalls). **Holding Risks** threaten the position independent of the Thesis (liquidity, position size relative to Portfolio, correlation with other Holdings). AQI tracks both, because a Thesis can be intact while the Holding is nonetheless imprudent at its current size — a distinction that connects Risk directly to Capital Allocation and Policy.

## Catalysts: dating the resolution of uncertainty

A Catalyst is an anticipated future event that will move Evidence for or against the Thesis. Where a Risk asks "what could go wrong," a Catalyst asks "when will we *know*." Catalysts give a Thesis a schedule of tests rather than leaving it to drift indefinitely.

A well-formed Catalyst records:
- **The event**: the specific anticipated occurrence (an earnings release, a product launch, a regulatory decision, a contract renewal, a capacity milestone).
- **Expected timing**: when it is anticipated, with an acknowledged uncertainty window.
- **What it would confirm or disconfirm**: the specific load-bearing assumption the event bears on, and *which outcome means what*. A Catalyst whose outcomes are not pre-mapped to Thesis implications is not usable — it must be defined such that "result A confirms assumption X; result B disconfirms it."
- **Directionality and magnitude**: whether resolution would strengthen, weaken, or decisively settle the Thesis.

Pre-committing the interpretation of a Catalyst *before* it resolves is the essential discipline. It prevents the after-the-fact rationalization in which any outcome is reinterpreted as bullish. When the Catalyst resolves, its outcome enters as high-weight Evidence, is promoted against the Thesis, and typically drives a Confirmation, Challenge, or Invalidation transition. The resolution — and whether it matched the pre-committed expectation — is captured as a Learning Event feeding the Behavior Profile (e.g., surfacing a pattern of systematically optimistic Catalyst forecasting).

## Monitoring Holdings over time

Risks and Catalysts are the instruments that turn a Portfolio from a static list into a monitored system. For each active Holding, AQI maintains a live register:
- The **Risk register**: every identified Risk with its current state and its materialization signals, matched continuously against incoming Observations.
- The **Catalyst calendar**: every anticipated event with its expected window and pre-mapped implications, surfacing upcoming resolutions.

This register is what makes monitoring *proactive* rather than reactive. Instead of noticing a problem when the price moves, the investor is watching for the specific, pre-named signals that a known Risk is materializing or that a known Catalyst is due. The Observation stream is filtered through this register: an Observation matching a Risk's materialization signal or a Catalyst's resolution is automatically flagged as high-relevance for promotion to Evidence.

At the Portfolio level, Risks aggregate. Individually acceptable Holding Risks can compound into a concentrated Portfolio-level exposure (correlated Risks across multiple Holdings, shared sensitivity to a single macro factor). AQI surfaces these aggregations so that Capital Allocation and Policy can respond — for instance, a Policy limiting combined exposure to a shared Risk factor. This connects the Risk register directly to sizing and diversification decisions.

## The discipline Risks and Catalysts enforce

The deeper purpose of tracking Risks and Catalysts is behavioral. Left unstructured, investors systematically underweight disconfirming futures and forget the timelines on which their theses were supposed to prove out. By requiring, at Formation, an explicit statement of *what could break this* and *when we will know*, AQI forces the uncomfortable analysis to the front — while the investor is still capable of it — and then holds them to it. When a Risk realizes or a Catalyst resolves against the Thesis, the pre-committed conditions make the required Decision unambiguous, removing the discretion that behavioral bias would otherwise exploit. Risks and Catalysts are, in this sense, the machinery that keeps AQI honestly adaptive.
