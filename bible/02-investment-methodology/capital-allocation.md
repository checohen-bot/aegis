# Capital Allocation

Individual Theses and Decisions concern single Companies. Capital allocation concerns the **Portfolio** as a whole — the collection of Holdings evaluated against a **Capital Mission**, the investor's overarching purpose, goals, and constraints for their capital (time horizon, risk tolerance, objectives, liquidity needs). AQI's stance on allocation is distinctive and follows directly from its philosophy: **sizing, diversification, and rebalancing are driven by the Capital Mission and by Thesis quality — not by market timing or price prediction.** The question is never "what will the market do next"; it is "given this investor's mission and the quality of these beliefs, how should capital be arranged."

## The Capital Mission as the allocation anchor

Every allocation Decision resolves against the Capital Mission. The Mission is not a vague preference; it is a structured set of parameters that make Portfolio-level judgments answerable:
- **Time horizon**: how long the capital can compound before it is needed. This governs how much illiquidity and short-term volatility the Portfolio can bear.
- **Risk tolerance**: both the capacity to sustain drawdowns (financial) and the willingness to (behavioral, informed by the Behavior Profile). These can diverge, and AQI treats the binding one as the constraint.
- **Objectives**: what the capital is *for* — growth, income, preservation, a specific future liability. Different objectives imply different acceptable Holding profiles.
- **Constraints**: liquidity requirements, tax situation, ethical exclusions, and any externally imposed limits.

Because allocation resolves against the Mission rather than against a market view, AQI produces allocation guidance that is *stable* — it does not lurch with sentiment. A change in allocation should trace to a change in the Mission, a change in a Thesis's quality, or a materialized Risk, never to a market forecast.

## Sizing: conviction and quality, bounded by mission

Position size in AQI is a function of two things: the **quality of the Thesis** (its Quality Framework profile — is the belief well-founded, well-evidenced, and falsifiable?) and the **fit to the Capital Mission** (does this Holding's risk and horizon match what the investor needs?). Size is *not* primarily a function of expected return, because expected return is a prediction and AQI does not stake sizing on predictive accuracy.

Concretely:
- A high-quality Thesis — falsifiable, load-bearing assumptions well-supported, strong business and management quality, low unresolved Risk — earns a larger allocation than a speculative one, because the *reasoning* is more trustworthy, not because the payoff is forecast to be larger.
- A Thesis with a wide, unresolved break-level Risk is sized down regardless of its upside, because the Holding's contribution to Portfolio-level risk is high.
- Size is always bounded by Policy: single-Holding limits and shared-Risk-factor limits cap any position irrespective of conviction. This prevents the most common catastrophic error — over-concentrating on a single belief, however good.

The sizing question AQI asks is therefore: *how much of this investor's mission-bound capital should be entrusted to the quality of this specific belief?*

## Diversification: managing Risk aggregation, not chasing count

AQI treats diversification as a tool for managing the aggregation of Risk across the Portfolio, not as a numeric target ("own 30 stocks"). The relevant object is the Portfolio-level Risk register: the set of Risks that materialize *together*. Holdings whose Theses depend on the same underlying Risk factor — the same macro sensitivity, the same regulatory regime, the same demand driver — are not diversified even if they are different Companies. AQI surfaces these correlated exposures so the investor sees true concentration rather than nominal spread.

Diversification is thus assessed by asking: *if this named Risk materializes, how much of the Portfolio is impaired at once?* A Portfolio of twenty Holdings all exposed to a single interest-rate regime is concentrated; a Portfolio of eight Holdings with genuinely independent Theses may be well-diversified. The Capital Mission's risk tolerance sets how much aggregate correlated exposure is acceptable, and Policy encodes the limit.

Over-diversification is also a failure mode AQI names: a Portfolio so spread that no Holding is large enough to matter is one where the investor cannot maintain a high-quality Thesis on each Holding. Diversification is bounded by the investor's genuine capacity to *know* what they own.

## Rebalancing: mission-driven, thesis-triggered

AQI rejects calendar-mechanical or market-timing rebalancing. Rebalancing is triggered by three principled events, all internal to the methodology:

1. **Thesis status change.** A Thesis moving to Invalidation mandates a resize or exit; a Thesis strengthening through Confirmation may justify a larger allocation. The Thesis Lifecycle, not the calendar, drives the trade.
2. **Drift from mission.** When Holdings appreciate or depreciate, the Portfolio's risk and concentration drift from what the Capital Mission specifies. Rebalancing restores alignment with the Mission — trimming a position that has grown past its Policy limit, for instance — independent of any view on where the price goes next.
3. **Mission change.** When the investor's own circumstances change (horizon shortens, liquidity need appears, objectives shift), the Capital Mission is updated and the Portfolio is re-aligned to it.

In every case the rebalancing rationale is recorded as a Decision, with its Thesis or Mission reference, so that Portfolio-level moves are as auditable and learnable as single-Holding ones.

## Portfolio-level quality

The Quality Framework applies at the Portfolio level, not only the Holding level. A Portfolio is high-quality when: every Holding is grounded in a documented, falsifiable Thesis; aggregate Risk exposure is within Mission tolerance; concentration is within Policy; and the whole is coherent with the stated objectives. AQI evaluates the Portfolio against these criteria continuously and surfaces the binding weakness — the Holding lacking a current Thesis, the correlated exposure breaching tolerance, the position that has drifted past its limit.

The result is a capital-allocation discipline that is entirely internal and controllable. It never asks the investor to predict the market. It asks only: *is this Portfolio a faithful, well-reasoned, well-diversified expression of this investor's mission?* That question the investor can always answer, and answering it well is the compounding lever AQI exists to pull.
