# The Decision Process

A **Decision** is a recorded choice — buy, sell, hold, or resize — made by the investor, together with its stated reasoning. In AQI the Decision is the pivotal object: it is the moment reasoning becomes action, and it is the unit whose *quality* the entire methodology exists to raise. This chapter defines what must exist before a Decision is legitimately made, how its reasoning is captured, and how Decisions relate to Policy constraints. The governing principle is simple: **a Decision's quality is judged by its inputs and process, not by its outcome.**

## Preconditions: what must exist before a Decision

AQI does not permit a well-formed Decision to be made in a vacuum. Before a buy, sell, or resize Decision is recorded, three things must exist and be referenced:

**1. A Thesis.** There must be an explicit, falsifiable Investment Thesis the Decision acts on. A buy expresses belief in a Thesis; a sell or resize expresses a change in a Thesis's status or in the Holding's fit. A Decision with no referenced Thesis is *ungrounded* and scores zero on Process Quality regardless of how it turns out — because it cannot be tested against anything and cannot feed the Learning Loop. "I bought it because it felt cheap" is not a Decision AQI can improve.

**2. A supporting Investment Case.** There must be a current Investment Case — the structured, evidence-backed argument — mapping Evidence to the Thesis's load-bearing assumptions, and including genuine disconfirming Evidence. The Case is what distinguishes a reasoned Decision from an impulse. Its Evidence Quality and completeness are surfaced at the moment of Decision.

**3. Considered Risks (and Catalysts).** The identified Risks that could break the Thesis, and the Catalysts that would resolve it, must have been reviewed. A Decision that ignores the Thesis's own named Risks is procedurally defective. For a sell or resize, the triggering Risk materialization or Catalyst resolution is part of the record.

These preconditions are not bureaucratic gates for their own sake. Each corresponds to a documented failure mode: acting without a falsifiable belief, acting without weighing evidence, and acting without considering what could go wrong. Requiring them at Decision time is how AQI raises Process Quality *before* the outcome is known.

## Capturing the reasoning

The defining feature of a Decision in AQI is that its reasoning is captured *at the moment it is made*, not reconstructed afterward. Memory is unreliable and, worse, self-serving — after an outcome, investors rewrite their prior reasoning to match what happened. Capturing reasoning contemporaneously defeats this hindsight distortion and is the raw material the Learning Loop requires.

A recorded Decision captures:
- **The action**: buy, sell, hold, or resize, with the size and its rationale.
- **The referenced Thesis and Case**: the specific belief and argument being acted on, at their current versions.
- **The decisive factors**: which elements of the Case — which Evidence, which Risk state, which Catalyst — actually drove the choice. This separates the load-bearing reasons from the incidental.
- **The disconfirming view considered**: what would have argued against this Decision, and why it was outweighed. A Decision recording no considered counter-argument is flagged as incomplete.
- **The behavioral check**: whether this Decision matches a known pattern in the investor's Behavior Profile (e.g., a familiar over-sizing impulse, a familiar tendency to average down into losers), and how that was addressed.
- **The expected outcome and its test**: what the investor expects to happen and by when — the pre-committed standard against which this Decision will later be evaluated as a Learning Event.

Capturing the *expected* outcome is essential and often omitted in ordinary practice. Without it, no Decision can later be scored honestly, because there is nothing to compare the actual outcome against. AQI makes the expectation part of the Decision record.

## Decisions and Policy

A **Policy** is a rule or guardrail the investor or the system applies to constrain future Decisions. Policies exist because in-the-moment reasoning is exactly when behavioral bias is strongest, and pre-committed rules are the most effective countermeasure. Policies typically encode:
- **Position and concentration limits**: maximum size for a single Holding, maximum combined exposure to a shared Risk factor or sector.
- **Process requirements**: e.g., a required Challenge before adding to a losing position; a cooling-off interval before acting on a sharp price move.
- **Behavioral guardrails**: rules derived from the investor's own Behavior Profile, targeting their documented failure modes.

At Decision time, AQI evaluates the proposed Decision against all applicable Policies. A Decision that violates a Policy is not silently blocked or silently permitted — it is *surfaced*. The investor may proceed, but doing so requires an explicit, recorded override with justification. This design preserves the investor's autonomy (Aegis advises; it does not trade) while ensuring that every departure from a pre-committed guardrail is a conscious, auditable act rather than a lapse. Overrides are themselves Evidence about the investor and feed the Behavior Profile — a pattern of overriding the same Policy is a finding.

## The Decision as a learning unit

Every Decision is written to close a loop. Because it captured its Thesis, its decisive factors, its considered counter-argument, its behavioral check, and its expected outcome, the Decision is fully evaluable once the outcome is known. When the outcome arrives, it is compared against the pre-committed expectation, and the difference — *whether the reasoning worked, and why* — is captured as a Learning Event. This is what allows AQI to score a Decision on process quality independent of luck: a Decision with sound inputs and a bad outcome is a *good Decision that was unlucky*, and it is retained as such, not punished. A Decision with unsound inputs and a good outcome is flagged as *lucky, not good* — the more dangerous case, because unexamined it will be repeated.

The Decision, in short, is where AQI's philosophy becomes operational: it is the point at which quality of reasoning is enforced through preconditions, made durable through contemporaneous capture, constrained through Policy, and rendered learnable through the pre-committed expectation. Everything upstream (Thesis, Case, Evidence, Risk) exists to make the Decision sound; everything downstream (Learning Event, Knowledge, Behavior Profile) exists to make the next one better.
