# The Learning Loop

The Learning Loop is where AQI's central claim — that decision quality compounds — becomes mechanical. Predictive edge does not compound: a correct guess teaches nothing transferable. Process quality compounds only if it is *fed back*: if the outcome of each Decision updates the reasoning that produces the next one. The Learning Loop is the machinery that does this feeding-back. Its core object is the **Learning Event**: a captured lesson from the outcome of a past Decision, feeding back into future reasoning. This chapter defines how Learning Events are generated, how they update Knowledge and the Behavior Profile, and how they accumulate into the institutional memory around which Aegis is built.

## Generating a Learning Event

A Learning Event is generated when a Decision's outcome can be compared against what the Decision expected. This is possible only because, per the Decision Process, every Decision recorded its pre-committed expected outcome and the test by which it would be judged. When the outcome materializes — a Catalyst resolves, a Risk realizes, a Thesis reaches Confirmation or Invalidation — the loop closes.

The generative act is a structured comparison, not a verdict on profit and loss. It asks:
- **What did the Decision expect, and what actually happened?**
- **Was the reasoning sound?** Judged on inputs and process (Was the Thesis falsifiable? Was the Evidence good? Were the Risks considered?), independent of the outcome.
- **What was the relationship between reasoning quality and outcome?** This produces one of four fundamental cases:
  - *Good reasoning, good outcome* — the process worked; reinforce it.
  - *Good reasoning, bad outcome* — an unlucky Decision; the process was sound and should be retained. This is the case ordinary investors wrongly punish, discarding good process because of variance.
  - *Bad reasoning, bad outcome* — the process failed; identify the specific defect.
  - *Bad reasoning, good outcome* — luck, not skill. This is the most dangerous case, because an unexamined win reinforces a flawed process that will eventually fail. AQI flags it explicitly.
- **What is the transferable lesson?** The specific, reusable insight — about the Company, the sector, a class of Thesis, or the investor's own conduct.

Separating reasoning quality from outcome is the single most important discipline in the loop. Without it, learning tracks luck and the investor is trained by noise. With it, the investor is trained by process — which is the only thing that carries forward.

## Updating Knowledge

**Knowledge** is durable, structured understanding accumulated over time about a Company, sector, or pattern. Learning Events feed Knowledge along several channels:

- **Company Knowledge.** A resolved Thesis about a Company deposits what was learned about that business — which assumptions proved sound, which management claims proved reliable, how the business actually behaved through the Catalyst. The next Thesis on that Company begins from this accumulated base rather than from zero.
- **Sector and pattern Knowledge.** Repeated Learning Events reveal patterns that transcend a single Company: a class of Thesis that reliably fails (e.g., "turnaround theses in this sector consistently underestimate customer-switching costs"), a category of Evidence that proves misleading, a type of Catalyst whose resolution is routinely misread. These become reusable priors that raise the quality of future Thesis Formation.
- **Methodological Knowledge.** The loop also learns about the framework itself — which Quality Framework criteria proved most predictive of good outcomes, which invalidation conditions were well-chosen. This refines how AQI is applied.

Knowledge is structured and attributed, not a heap of notes. Each Knowledge item traces to the Learning Events and retired Theses that produced it, so its basis is auditable and it can be revised when later Evidence contradicts it. Knowledge is *durable* precisely because it is decoupled from any single live position — retired Theses persist, and their lessons remain available long after the Holding is gone.

## Updating the Behavior Profile

**Behavior Profile** is a model of the investor's own decision-making tendencies and biases, built from their history. Where Knowledge is about the world, the Behavior Profile is about the investor. It is updated by the subset of Learning Events that reveal something about *how this person reasons and errs*.

The loop mines Decisions and their outcomes for recurring patterns in conduct:
- Systematic over- or under-sizing relative to Thesis quality.
- Averaging down into invalidated Theses (failing to honor invalidation conditions).
- Systematically optimistic Catalyst timing or magnitude.
- Repeatedly overriding a particular Policy.
- Selling confirmed Theses too early, or holding invalidated ones too long.
- Characteristic Evidence blind spots — e.g., consistently underweighting disconfirming Evidence for favored Companies.

Each identified tendency updates the Behavior Profile, and the Profile in turn feeds back into the live process: it powers the behavioral check at Decision time, informs which Policies are proposed as guardrails, and determines when a Thesis should be routed into Challenge because the investor is in a situation where they historically err. The loop thus adapts not only the investor's view of each Company but the system's model of the investor's own failure modes — and deploys that self-knowledge as a control.

## Compounding into institutional memory

The strategic payoff of the Learning Loop is **institutional memory**: the accumulated, structured, attributed record of every Thesis, Decision, outcome, lesson, and behavioral finding, available to inform every future Decision. This is what makes Aegis a memory rather than a forecasting engine, and it is where the compounding claim of AQI is realized.

The compounding is concrete. Each pass through the loop raises the quality of the next Thesis Formation (better priors from Knowledge), the next Decision (sharper behavioral checks from the Behavior Profile), and the next Policy (guardrails tuned to demonstrated failure modes). Because the substrate is *reasoning and conduct* — not predictions — the gains persist and accumulate; they do not wash out with market variance. An investor ten years into using AQI is not a better forecaster. They are the beneficiary of a decade of structured lessons about the businesses they follow, the Thesis patterns that work, and their own characteristic mistakes — a compounding advantage no single prediction could ever provide.

That is the closing of AQI's argument: quality is the controllable lever, and the Learning Loop is the mechanism by which quality, uniquely, compounds.
