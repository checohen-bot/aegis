# Non-Goals

A mission is defined as much by its refusals as its ambitions. These are ours — written down, explicit, and permanent, because every one of them will be tempting someday. Some already are. When the tempting day comes, this document is the answer, and the burden of proof sits entirely on whoever wants to change it.

## We are not a trading bot

Aegis will never autonomously trade user capital, and will never be built to shrink the space between impulse and execution. The entire product exists to strengthen human judgment; automated trading replaces it. The moment we execute unreviewed decisions, our incentives decouple from our mission — we'd inherit the trading industry's metrics (volume, frequency, latency) and every one of them corrodes decision quality. Execution through our IBKR integration flows from a reasoned, human-owned investment case. The human decides. Always. This is not a current-phase limitation; it is the identity of the company.

## We are not a prediction engine

Aegis will never emit price targets, forecast market direction, or score stocks by expected short-term movement. Not because prediction wouldn't sell — it demonstrably sells — but because it is intellectually dishonest at retail scale, and the entire company rests on the premise that decision quality, not predictive accuracy, drives long-term returns. Shipping even one predictive feature would put two contradictory epistemologies in one product, and the flashy dishonest one would slowly eat the rigorous honest one. It always does. We win by making the user a better reasoner, not by pretending to see the future.

## We are not a predictive black box — nothing unexplainable ships

Beyond refusing prediction, we refuse opacity in any form. Any output that cannot unfold into its evidence and reasoning does not ship, no matter how accurate it appears in testing. A black box that happens to be right is more dangerous than one that's visibly wrong — it teaches trust it cannot honor, and its failures arrive without warning or diagnosis. "The model said so" is not an explanation; it is the absence of one. This is a hard product gate, not an aspiration: explainability is a precondition for existing in the Aegis product at all.

## We are not a chatbot

Conversation is an interface; it is not the product, and it will never be the product's center. "Chat with an AI about stocks" produces pleasant, ephemeral, unaccountable talk — nothing accumulates, nothing is falsifiable, nothing is auditable. Aegis produces *artifacts*: theses, evidence chains, investment cases, decision records — structured objects that persist, version, and compound into institutional memory. Where conversational interaction serves the building of those artifacts, we'll use it gladly. But a transcript is not a thesis, and a company that confuses the two builds an entertainment product with a finance vocabulary.

## We are not a stock screener

Screeners answer "what matches these filters?" — a commodity question answered free in a hundred places, and a workflow that quietly teaches the worst habit in investing: starting from the stock instead of the business. Business First means understanding precedes selection. Filtering may someday exist in service of research workflows, but discovery-by-filter will never be the product's spine, and we will never compete on screening criteria count. That is a race to the bottom of a mountain we're not climbing.

## We are not a brokerage

We will not hold custody of user assets or become the execution venue. Brokerage economics are engagement economics — revenue that scales with activity — and they are precisely the incentive structure that broke this industry for individual investors. By integrating with Interactive Brokers instead of replacing it, we keep our incentives pointed at exactly one thing: the quality of the user's decisions. The moment we profit per-trade, our interests and our users' interests diverge, and no amount of principle-writing survives a business model that pays us to betray it.

## We are not built on any single AI model or vendor

No Aegis capability will ever be architecturally bound to GPT, Claude, Gemini, or any successor. AI capabilities are permanent; specific models are replaceable infrastructure — evaluated per task, benchmarked on merit, swapped without drama when the frontier moves. Vendor lock-in would hand our roadmap, our cost structure, and ultimately our users' trust to a third party's release calendar. Our moat must live in what no model swap can touch: the reasoning discipline, the evidence architecture, the accumulated institutional memory. If our advantage could be neutralized by a competitor calling the same API, it was never an advantage.

## We are not chasing institutions or day traders

Two customer sets we permanently decline. Institutions would pull the roadmap toward compliance suites, multi-seat workflows, and sales-driven feature bloat — and their money is loud; it always ends up steering. Day traders optimize for exactly what we consider noise; serving them means building speed-and-signal features that are poison to a decision-quality platform. Every product decision at Aegis is made for one person: the serious individual investor building long-term wealth through reasoning. A platform that serves everyone's relationship with the market serves no one's well.

## We are not expanding beyond public equities until public equities are proven

No options, no crypto, no forex, no fixed income, no private markets — until Aegis is demonstrably the best reasoning environment in the world for public equities, full stop. Every asset class demands its own analytical depth, data foundations, and evidence patterns; premature breadth converts a rigorous platform into a shallow one asset class by asset class. Public equities are where business-first analysis lives most naturally, where our users actually build long-term wealth, and where our philosophy can be proven end to end. Expansion is earned by excellence, not granted by ambition. The roadmap pressure to go wide will be constant. The answer is in this file.

---

Each refusal protects the same three assets: the user's decision quality, the coherence of the product, and the trust we're compounding. Strategy is sacrifice. Companies rarely die from refusing opportunities; they die from accepting the ones that dissolve what made them matter. This list is how Aegis stays Aegis when saying yes would be easier.

The mission defines what we build. This document guards it.
